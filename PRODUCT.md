# Product

## Audience
People who want live wind, rain, and radar at a glance, mostly on a phone (often an iPhone), mostly in the US. The default place is Dunnellon / Ocala, FL, used whenever geolocation is denied or unavailable.

## Purpose
Open the app and see the weather moving over a satellite map right away. Your location is a blue dot. Drag the map and the readout follows whatever is under the center cross; tap the locate button to snap back to you. Search any city or street address. Tap the readout to open a full weather page whose live background matches the weather there.

## Constraints
- Single screen with one full-screen page on top of it. No splash, no onboarding, no accounts, no push notifications. The map is the app.
- Website first. The phone is the priority (portrait, one-handed, safe-area insets); desktop must also work well.
- Look and feel: two themes over one layout (owner, 2026-10-05). **Liquid** (default) is close to Apple's own apps: liquid glass, SF Pro on Apple devices, system colors, live skies. **Classic** keeps the original black-and-amber style. The Look control at the bottom of the Layers drawer switches; the choice is remembered. `DESIGN.md` is the source of truth.
- Layer and data logic stays framework-free so a later native app (Swift or a wrapped port) can reuse or mirror it.
- Selected point: either "you" (GPS, blue dot, locate button filled) or "the map center" (after any drag, tap, or search; the center cross shows). The readout reloads when the map settles, not on every frame. The pin in the weather card's corner (owner, 2026-10-09: one tap, nothing buried) is on by default (owner, 2026-10-09: the weather stays on your location unless you turn it off): the readout stays on the place it shows (you, a searched place, or the point under the cross), a pin marks it on the map unless it's you, and moving or tapping the map changes nothing until you search or tap locate. Tapped off, the weather follows the map: the center cross shows and the readout is whatever is under it. Hovering the pin says what it does right away. There is no separate Settings page: units, thunder sound, the look, and the data sources are one short group at the bottom of the Layers drawer.
- Search: cities and street addresses, suggestions as you type, nearby results first. Empty, it lists My places (owner, 2026-10-29: Apple's list of your cities): where you are first, then your tagged places, newest first, each with its sky, temperature, condition, and high and low (one request for all, kept ten minutes); tap one to go there, × to remove it.
- Tagged places (owner, 2026-10-10): press and hold the map (right-click with a mouse) to tag the spot with a name (the nearest town to start, editable), or tap Tag on a place's weather page. Each shows on the map as a pin that grows as you zoom in, labeled with its name; tapping it flies there and shows its weather under that name. Press and hold a pin to rename or remove it; the search list removes them too. Tags are kept on the device (no accounts), up to 50.

- Timeline: one play bar scrubs or plays radar from 24 hours ago, through now, into the HRRR forecast (as far as the latest run reaches, about 15–18 hours), in 15-minute frames. Playback is smooth: storms glide along their tracked motion between frames (owner, 2026-10-09), and the last 2 hours play MRMS's real frames about 5 minutes apart, with edges that move rather than pictures that fade through each other (owner, 2026-10-09: the motion "felt cheap"); frames load ahead, and playback waits rather than skip, but smoothly (owner, 2026-10-11: "play smooth as butter and scrub back and forth smooth as butter"): it starts once a few frames are in and eases up to speed, eases its speed down rather than freezing when the network falls behind, and plays a zoom coarser (sharpening when it stops) while full-resolution tiles can't keep up; the slow satellite estimate never holds it up (a nearby estimate stands in until each frame's arrives). Scrubbing follows the finger continuously, gliding between frames, anywhere on the timeline: a coarse preview of every frame loads in the background (while nothing plays), stands in at once, and sharpens when the thumb rests; letting go settles on the nearest frame. The opening loop stays loaded whole, on any screen size, so it plays without stopping to reload every lap (owner, 2026-10-11: it played, froze, and played again). Zooming or panning keeps the rain moving the same way (owner, 2026-10-10: the radar rippled like water at the screen's edges while zooming): its motion is measured when the frames change or the map settles, not on every frame of a gesture. Now and the last 2 hours come from NOAA's MRMS mosaic (cleaner: clutter and birds removed; about 2 minutes old), older hours from IEM's archive of MRMS (owner, 2026-10-09; MRMS since 2026-10-11, when the raw NEXRAD composite showed the evening's bird and insect migration as a green sheet over the land for the frames just before MRMS took over). Radar is smoothed and recolored to read like TV-weather radar. Wind and falling-rain particles follow the nearest hour. - Traffic cameras (owner, 2026-10-12: "a main button for traffic cameras that overlay onto the map, and click it to bring it up and watch it live"): a camera button in the top-right column puts state DOTs' cameras on the map (off on first load; cities' hundreds group into counts until you zoom in). Tapping one opens it in the sheet: live video where the DOT streams it to any site (California, Nevada, Wisconsin, Louisiana), otherwise the DOT's snapshot, renewed as often as the DOT does (every 10 seconds to 2 minutes), with a link to watch live on the DOT's own site (Florida, Georgia, and Pennsylvania keep their video to their sites with a token and a referrer check; the app doesn't get around that). Several views per camera where the DOT has them, and the cameras nearby, so you can follow a road. Cameras from 13 sources, about 19,000: the 511 sites of Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Arizona, Utah, Nevada, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, northern New England, Idaho, and Alaska, and Caltrans.
- Trip weather: pick a start (your location by default) and a destination, leave now or up to a week later, and see what the drive runs into at the time you will be at each spot: NWS warnings, watches, and advisories the route crosses (and whether they are in effect while you pass), SPC severe-storm and WPC flash-flood outlooks, and each stop's forecast for its arrival hour (thunderstorms, heavy rain, snow, ice, fog, strong gusts). Stops are every 30 minutes of driving (hourly past 36 hours). Tapping a stop shows it on the map with forecast radar for that hour when the timeline reaches it. Hovering a stop's dot on the map (tapping it on a phone) shows a card: when you'll arrive, the forecast then, and any watches or warnings over that spot at that time (owner, 2026-10-09). Long drives split into days: up to 8, 10 (default), or 12 hours of driving a day, or nonstop; the days are balanced (the fewest that fit, equal length), each night falls where a day ends, and each morning starts at 7, 8 (default), or 9 AM in that town's own time zone, after at least 8 hours. Nights show the evening and morning forecast, and alerts count for the whole stay. Times read in each place's local time, marked when it differs from the phone's. Drive times are typical speeds with no breaks.
- Weather maps (owner, 2026-10-06, after Windy): one colored variable at a time under borders, radar, wind, and labels — wind, gusts, temperature, feels like, rain, rain over the next 24 hours, thunderstorm energy (CAPE), clouds, low clouds, humidity, dew point, pressure, fog/visibility, waves, swell, ocean currents, sea temperature, air quality (U.S. AQI), snow depth, new snow, and infrared satellite — or none (the default). Drawn smooth, they follow the timeline (past hours from earlier model runs, the forecast ahead; infrared shows the last 24 hours) and crossfade between hours. A legend under the timeline shows the scale in the user's units and the value at the selected point. The choice is remembered.
- Wind particles are short comets like Windy's: a tapered streak along the wind, longer where it's stronger, fading in and out (owner, 2026-10-06). ## Voice
- Plain and short. Present tense. Sentence case. Apple-style card headers in caps ("HOURLY FORECAST").
- The condition line is 12 words or fewer.
- Numbers first: "72°", "H:87° L:76°", "Wind SE 9 mph".
- No exclamation marks, no hype, no "Oops". Errors state what happened and what still works.
- Approximations are labelled as approximate.

- 2026-10-05: the owner disliked the black-and-amber style and asked for a redesign close to Apple's UI: liquid glass, a weather-matching background behind the menu, a blue location dot, a clear GPS button, a center icon that picks the point while dragging, a city/address search bar, and more expensive, crisp, soft typography.
- No user research yet. Unknown: real usage split between phone and desktop; assumed phone-first.

The iPhone app (owner, 2026-10-24: get it on the App Store): this same site wrapped with Capacitor (`ios/`), iPhone only and portrait like Apple Weather, dark, edge to edge like the home-screen app, with location from iOS (one permission prompt, not the web view's two). A privacy policy page (`public/privacy.html`) is linked from Layers → Settings and is the listing's privacy URL. README → iPhone app has the build steps.

The weather page's cards darken with the sky behind them (owner, 2026-10-26: white type on light cards over bright clouds was hard to read): the sky reports how bright it is (its own colors, and its clouds by how much they cover), and the cards go from a light tint at night to a deeper blue-gray under a bright overcast, the gray type brightening with them, so text stays readable on any sky.
- 2026-10-29: the weather page gained what other apps have (owner: "build all that in strategically… still very clean, very simple, while still not sacrificing any data"): an Averages tile (Apple's: today's high against the ten-year average high for the date, with today, average, and yesterday's high), a Pollen tile where it's forecast (CAMS covers Europe only; free U.S. pollen data doesn't exist), a Tides card on the coast (the nearest NOAA station within 12 miles: the water now, rising or falling, the next high and low, and today's curve), and a Forecast models card (the European, American, and German models' high and chance of rain for each of the ten days, so you can see where they agree). Each loads when the page shows a place, is kept an hour, and shows only where there's something to show.
- 2026-10-29: fire and flood, smoke, and the aurora (same request): Layers → Weather has a third group, Fire and flood, each switch pinnable to the bar like the others: Wildfires (NIFC's burning fires, not fully contained ones or prescribed burns, as orange dots sized by acres, the ones under 100 acres only from zoom 6, named from zoom 7, with their mapped edges; tap one for its size, containment, when it was found, cause, and crews) and River flooding (NOAA's river gauges in NWS flood colors, purple major to yellow near flood stage, those at or near flood stage everywhere and, from zoom 8, every gauge in view; tap one for the river now against its flood stages and a link to its forecast). The weather maps gain Wildfire smoke (Canada's FireWork forecast of smoke near the ground, North America, hourly through the timeline) and Aurora (NOAA's 30-to-90-minute forecast of the chance of one overhead, the same at every moment, since there's no history).
