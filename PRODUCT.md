# Product

## Audience
People who want live wind, rain, and radar at a glance, mostly on a phone (often an iPhone), mostly in the US. The default place is Dunnellon / Ocala, FL, used whenever geolocation is denied or unavailable.

## Purpose
Open the app and see the weather moving over a satellite map right away. Your location is a blue dot. Drag the map and the readout follows whatever is under the center cross; tap the locate button to snap back to you. Search any city or street address. Tap the readout to open a full weather page whose live background matches the weather there.

## Constraints
- Single screen with one full-screen page on top of it. No splash, no onboarding, no accounts, no push notifications. The map is the app.
- Website first. The phone is the priority (portrait, one-handed, safe-area insets); desktop must also work well.
- Look and feel: two themes over one layout (owner, 2026-10-05). **Liquid** (default) is close to Apple's own apps: liquid glass, SF Pro on Apple devices, system colors, live skies. **Classic** keeps the original black-and-amber style. The Look control at the bottom of the Layers drawer switches; the choice is remembered. `DESIGN.md` is the source of truth.
- Layer and data logic stays framework-free so a later native app (Swift or a wrapped port) can reuse or mirror it.
- Selected point: either "you" (GPS, blue dot, locate button filled) or "the map center" (after any drag, tap, or search; the center cross shows). The readout reloads when the map settles, not on every frame. The pin in the weather card's corner (owner, 2026-10-09: one tap, nothing buried) switches "weather follows the map" (the default, the owner's original design) off: the readout stays on the place it shows (you, a searched place, or the point under the cross), a pin marks it on the map, and moving or tapping the map changes nothing until you tap the pin again, search, or tap locate. There is no separate Settings page: units, thunder sound, the look, and the data sources are one short group at the bottom of the Layers drawer.
- Search: cities and street addresses, suggestions as you type, nearby results first.
- Layers live in one drawer on the right, like Windy's menu (owner, 2026-10-06): first the switches, in two groups — what's happening now (Radar, Satellite clouds, Lightning, Wind) and storms (Storm tracks, Alert areas, Storm outlook, Hurricanes; owner, 2026-10-09) — then the weather map, picked from thumbnails, then map style, radar colors, and falling rain. A hurricane tracker button sits in the top-right controls: on shows storms and opens the storm panel; off hides them. Below it, lightning only (owner, 2026-10-09) shows just radar and live lightning, hurricane tracking and everything else off, and puts the layers back on a second tap. Below that, map only hides every control to just look at the map (fullscreen where the browser allows; one fading button, Escape, or F brings the controls back). First load: Satellite map, Color radar, Radar and Wind on, falling rain off. Layer switches reset each visit; map style, colors, units, and sound are remembered.
- Timeline: one play bar scrubs or plays radar from 24 hours ago, through now, into the HRRR forecast (as far as the latest run reaches, about 15–18 hours), in 15-minute frames. Playback is smooth: storms glide along their tracked motion between frames (owner, 2026-10-09), frames load ahead, and playback waits rather than skip. Now and the last 2 hours come from NOAA's MRMS mosaic (cleaner: clutter and birds removed; about 2 minutes old), older hours from the NEXRAD archive (owner, 2026-10-09). Beyond radar range a satellite rain estimate fills in, so a hurricane's core offshore isn't blank; the first forecast hour continues from the radar now and hands off to the model, so crossing "now" doesn't jump; colors follow The Weather Channel's look, the owner's reference (2026-10-09). Snow, mix, and ice are colored apart from rain, from the HRRR's precipitation type (owner, 2026-10-09). Satellite clouds (beta) is a main layer: live GOES imagery with the ground and sea cut away, so the clouds lie on the map in place, worldwide (GOES where it sees, NOAA's global mosaic for the Atlantic and beyond) (owner, 2026-10-09; first a hurricane-tracker option, moved up the same day). Radar is smoothed and recolored to read like TV-weather radar. Wind and falling-rain particles follow the nearest hour. On open, the radar loops from an hour ago to an hour ahead at half speed until the timeline is touched (pause, play, scrub, or keys); not when the device asks for reduced motion.
- Trip weather: pick a start (your location by default) and a destination, leave now or up to a week later, and see what the drive runs into at the time you will be at each spot: NWS warnings, watches, and advisories the route crosses (and whether they are in effect while you pass), SPC severe-storm and WPC flash-flood outlooks, and each stop's forecast for its arrival hour (thunderstorms, heavy rain, snow, ice, fog, strong gusts). Stops are every 30 minutes of driving (hourly past 36 hours). Tapping a stop shows it on the map with forecast radar for that hour when the timeline reaches it. Hovering a stop's dot on the map (tapping it on a phone) shows a card: when you'll arrive, the forecast then, and any watches or warnings over that spot at that time (owner, 2026-10-09). Long drives split into days: up to 8, 10 (default), or 12 hours of driving a day, or nonstop; the days are balanced (the fewest that fit, equal length), each night falls where a day ends, and each morning starts at 7, 8 (default), or 9 AM in that town's own time zone, after at least 8 hours. Nights show the evening and morning forecast, and alerts count for the whole stay. Times read in each place's local time, marked when it differs from the phone's. Drive times are typical speeds with no breaks.
- Weather maps (owner, 2026-10-06, after Windy): one colored variable at a time under borders, radar, wind, and labels — wind, gusts, temperature, feels like, rain, rain over the next 24 hours, thunderstorm energy (CAPE), clouds, low clouds, humidity, dew point, pressure, fog/visibility, waves, swell, ocean currents, sea temperature, air quality (U.S. AQI), snow depth, new snow, and infrared satellite — or none (the default). Drawn smooth, they follow the timeline (past hours from earlier model runs, the forecast ahead; infrared shows the last 24 hours) and crossfade between hours. A legend under the timeline shows the scale in the user's units and the value at the selected point. The choice is remembered.
- Wind particles are short comets like Windy's: a tapered streak along the wind, longer where it's stronger, fading in and out (owner, 2026-10-06). Their wind is Environment Canada's global model (15 km) through GeoMet; Open-Meteo's lattice is fetched only for falling rain and lightning (both off by default), or for wind if GeoMet is down.
- Place names follow U.S. usage: the basemap's "Gulf of Mexico" / "Golfo de México" reads "Gulf of America", on the map and in search (owner, 2026-10-06).
- Weather page: alerts, next 2 hours of rain, hourly, 7 days, and detail tiles (feels like, UV, wind, humidity, sun, visibility, pressure, precipitation) over a live sky that matches current conditions and time of day.
- Desktop (1100px and wider): the weather page docks on the right. Keyboard: Space plays/pauses, ←/→ step an hour, Home returns to now, `/` focuses search, F toggles map only.
- Map shows active NWS alert areas (warnings and watches, not marine or minor advisories) in the NWS hazard colors, and an alerts button counts those in view and lists them by type, most dangerous first, down to each alert's full text (owner, 2026-10-08, after WeatherWise).
- Single-site radar (owner, 2026-10-08, after WeatherWise): tap a NEXRAD tower to see its own scans, full resolution, for the past 24 hours and now (the forecast stays the national model), in reflectivity or storm-relative velocity; new scans arrive every few minutes. The close button returns to the national composite.
- Storm tracks (on by default): strong storm cells from the radars' own storm tracking, each with where it's headed and colored by its worst threat (tornado signature, rotation, large hail); tap one for its details. Shallow cells (tops under 8,000 ft, almost always ground clutter) and impossible motions are left out. Optional storm reports (tornado, hail, wind, flood) from the past 24 hours.
- Storm outlook layer: SPC's severe-storm or WPC's flash-flood outlook for today, tomorrow, or day 3, each area labeled with its risk.
- The map's position lives in the address bar (#map=zoom/lat/lon): reloads and shared links open there; without one the map opens where you last were (or on your last known location while a fresh fix comes in), never waiting on GPS.
- Speed: the basemap style loads alongside everything else, connections to the data hosts are warmed up front, the hurricane layers load after the first frame (and only the parts switched on), the trip planner downloads on first use, and a service worker keeps the app's files so repeat visits start from the device.
- Dark mode only.
- Free data, no API keys: NWS api.weather.gov (place, observations, forecast, alerts), NOAA MRMS via NCEP's GeoServer (radar now and the last 2 hours), Iowa Environmental Mesonet (archived NEXRAD composite, HRRR forecast radar, single sites), Open-Meteo (wind and precipitation fields, point forecast; CC BY 4.0), Photon by Komoot (search and place names; OpenStreetMap data), OSRM on FOSSGIS servers (driving routes; OpenStreetMap data), NOAA SPC and WPC outlooks (via NOAA map services), NHC tropical GIS (via NOAA map services) and NHC's storm list and ATCF model guidance (published by a scheduled GitHub Actions job to the repo's data branch, since those files can't be fetched from a browser), NOAA nowCOAST (GOES clouds), NHC storm graphics and NOAA STAR GOES floaters (storm panel images), NASA GIBS (sea surface temperature; GOES band 13 infrared for the infrared map), Environment and Climate Change Canada's MSC GeoMet (raw global model grids for the weather maps: GDPS 15 km, GDWPS waves, GIOPS ocean; attribution required), Esri World Imagery (satellite basemap). Attribution is always on screen.
- Apple Maps (MapKit) belongs in the future native iPhone app, where it is free and built in. On the web it would need a paid Apple Developer account and a map-engine swap.
- Open-Meteo free tier is 10k calls/day, 5k/hour, 600/minute, non-commercial, and every point in a multi-point request counts as a call. So the app asks it only for what no gridded source has: the selected point's forecast, and lattices only while falling rain, lightning, or an Open-Meteo weather map is on.
- Lightning is live (owner, 2026-10-08, after WeatherWise): GOES lightning mapper flashes, minute by minute, as a glow where lightning is happening with animated bolts and a crackle inside it. Approximate lightning from forecast thunderstorm cells stands in only if the live feed is down, and a note says so.
- Hurricanes (added at the owner's request, 2026-10-06, replacing the original "no hurricane UI"): while storms are active, a pill under the readout ("Nine · Depression", or "2 tropical systems") and, on the map, NHC's official cone, forecast track and points (colored by Saffir–Simpson category), coastal watches and warnings, past track, the seven-day outlook areas with their odds, and spaghetti models from NHC's ATCF guidance (official, consensus, hurricane models, global models, ensemble means, statistical, and the 31 GFS ensemble members). A storm panel shows wind, pressure, movement, storm-centered GOES satellite (color or infrared, with an on-request loop), NHC's forecast, an intensity chart, a switch per model group, and NHC's standard graphics. Hurricanes is a layer switch, on by default, with a disclosure listing what it shows so people can look at only the parts they want (cone only, spaghetti only…): forecast cone, track, spaghetti models and their ensemble members, past track, warnings, current wind field, five-day wind-speed odds (39+, 58+, 74+ mph), most likely arrival of storm winds, potential storm surge flooding when NHC issues it, the development outlook, and sea temperature. Choices are remembered.
- Out of scope: light mode, accounts, notifications, onboarding.

## Voice
- Plain and short. Present tense. Sentence case. Apple-style card headers in caps ("HOURLY FORECAST").
- The condition line is 12 words or fewer.
- Numbers first: "72°", "H:87° L:76°", "Wind SE 9 mph".
- No exclamation marks, no hype, no "Oops". Errors state what happened and what still works.
- Approximations are labelled as approximate.

## Evidence
- The brief: "open to see live wind + rain on a dark map, tap icons to toggle layers, read temp/conditions."
- Follow-up from the owner: build the website first, phone is the priority, port to a native app later.
- The owner wants radar colors (green to red), the falling rain off by default, and satellite (aerial and live clouds).
- The owner asked for overnight stops in the trip planner (2026-10-06).
- The owner asked for full hurricane tracking with spaghetti models from official sources, pointing at spaghettimodels.com as inspiration, and for richer weather-page backgrounds (2026-10-06). Then for a dropdown to turn each hurricane part on and off, and more of Mike's Weather Page (2026-10-06).
- The owner asked to bring over WeatherWise's most important features in this app's simpler style, and to be quicker than both (2026-10-08).
- The owner asked for Windy-style layers on the right and its colored maps, for cleaner wind that fades out like Windy's, and for "Gulf of America" on the map (2026-10-06).
- The owner reported the wind changing direction when zooming; fixed by an anchored, nested sampling lattice and time-blended hourly fields (2026-10-06).
- The owner asked for a trip planner: enter a route across the state or country and see advisories, flooding, and severe storms along it, timed to when you will be at each spot (2026-10-06).
- The owner asked for the radar to autoplay on open (an hour back to an hour ahead, looping until paused), and for a way to keep the weather on their location while browsing the map (2026-10-06).
- The owner found the radar blocky and asked for it smooth, like The Weather Channel's overlay; and wind lines shorter so they don't take over the map.
- The owner shared screenshots of MyRadar, (Not Boring) Weather, Apple Weather, and another app; take/skip decisions are in `docs/inspiration.md`.
- 2026-10-05: the owner disliked the black-and-amber style and asked for a redesign close to Apple's UI: liquid glass, a weather-matching background behind the menu, a blue location dot, a clear GPS button, a center icon that picks the point while dragging, a city/address search bar, and more expensive, crisp, soft typography.
- No user research yet. Unknown: real usage split between phone and desktop; assumed phone-first.
